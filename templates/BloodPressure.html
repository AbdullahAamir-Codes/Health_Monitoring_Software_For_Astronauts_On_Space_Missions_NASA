Blood pressure is one of the most important indicators of human health. It measures the force exerted by circulating blood against the walls of blood vessels. On Earth, gravity plays an important role in determining how blood is distributed throughout the body.

However, when astronauts travel into space, they experience microgravity, an environment in which the effects of gravity are greatly reduced. This causes significant changes in the way blood and other fluids move around the body.

As a result, astronauts can experience changes in blood pressure, heart function and fluid distribution. Monitoring these changes is essential to maintaining astronauts' health and ensuring their safety during space missions.

On Earth, gravity naturally pulls blood and body fluids towards the lower parts of the body, particularly the legs and feet. In space, microgravity reduces this gravitational effect. Fluids that would normally accumulate in the lower body begin shifting towards the upper body, including the chest, neck and head. This phenomenon is known as Fluid Shift.

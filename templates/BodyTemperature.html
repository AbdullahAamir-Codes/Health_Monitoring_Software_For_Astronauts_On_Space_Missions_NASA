Maintaining a stable body temperature is essential for astronaut health and survival during space missions. On Earth, the human body naturally regulates its internal temperature at approximately 37°C (98.6°F). However, living in a microgravity environment can affect the body's natural temperature regulation mechanisms.

During spaceflight, astronauts may experience changes in core body temperature, particularly during physical exercise and long-duration missions. Research conducted aboard the International Space Station (ISS) has shown that core body temperature can increase more rapidly during exercise in microgravity than it does on Earth.

Our Body Temperature Monitoring System is designed to help monitor astronauts' temperature, identify unusual fluctuations and provide early warnings of potential overheating or abnormal temperature conditions.

Astronaut body temperature can fluctuate depending on physical activity, environmental conditions, sleep patterns and physiological adaptation to microgravity.

The typical human core body temperature is approximately 37°C.

During spaceflight, astronauts may experience an increase in core body temperature, particularly during exercise or prolonged exposure to microgravity.

Excessive increases in core body temperature may indicate overheating or a potential medical concern requiring further assessment.

A decrease in body temperature may indicate excessive heat loss or difficulties maintaining normal thermal balance.

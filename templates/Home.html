Astronaut Mood

Astronauts in space may experience a range of emotions, from excitement and happiness to stress, loneliness, and homesickness. Being away from family and Earth for long periods can affect their mood, making emotional well-being and mental health monitoring essential during space missions.

Immune System

Space travel can weaken an astronaut’s immune system due to microgravity, radiation exposure, and stress. These conditions can reduce immune cell activity, making astronauts more vulnerable to infections and viruses during long-duration space missions.

Gravity

Gravity is the force that attracts objects towards each other, keeping planets and moons in orbit. In space, astronauts experience microgravity, where they appear weightless. Alternating gravity can simulate changing gravitational conditions, helping astronauts adapt to different gravity levels during space missions.

Health Risks

Astronauts face several health risks in space, including muscle and bone loss, radiation exposure, weakened immunity, vision changes, and sleep disturbances. Microgravity and isolation can also affect their physical and mental well-being during long-duration missions.
